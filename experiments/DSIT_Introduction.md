# 1. Introduction

Customer retention is a cornerstone of sustainable profitability in modern e-commerce. As customer acquisition costs continue to rise, identifying "at-risk" customers before they definitively defect—commonly known as churn prediction—has become a paramount objective for predictive analytics. Traditionally, e-commerce platforms have relied heavily on historical transactional data, utilizing behavioral proxies such as Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) metrics to forecast future engagement. While these behavioral models have achieved widespread adoption, they remain fundamentally constrained: they can only react to a decline in purchasing behavior that has already occurred, rather than proactively detecting the underlying shift in customer satisfaction that causes the decline.

Simultaneously, the proliferation of unstructured textual data—such as customer reviews and feedback—has presented a massive, largely untapped reservoir of predictive information. The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) has revolutionized our ability to extract nuanced, context-aware semantic insights from this text. Intuitively, integrating NLP-derived sentiments into churn prediction models should yield significant performance improvements. However, existing literature reveals a paradoxical reality: when advanced textual features are appended to traditional survival analysis models, they frequently fail to provide meaningful predictive gains over simple behavioral baselines. 

This paper argues that this widespread failure is not due to the inherent noise of textual data, but rather a profound **structural flaw** in how the e-commerce churn problem is traditionally formulated. Standard frameworks define the "churn event" retrospectively based on prolonged inactivity from a static snapshot date. Consequently, the input feature *Recency* becomes tautologically entangled with the target variable. This structural "Target Leakage" creates a state of **Behavioral Dominance**—an information saturation point where the model's predictive capacity is entirely consumed by trivial behavioral proxies, leaving no mathematical space for semantic variables to contribute. 

To resolve this methodological bottleneck, we propose the **Universal Survival Framework**, a forward-looking, episodic architecture that fundamentally decouples the churn definition from historical transactional proxies. By evaluating customer lifecycles strictly relative to sequences of interaction episodes (reviews), our framework establishes a rigorous, unbiased baseline. On top of this architecture, we integrate a novel semantic plugin utilizing LLM-derived sentiment trajectories—such as `semantic_variance` and `sentiment_flip_rate`—capturing the volatile dynamics of customer satisfaction immediately preceding dormancy.

To systematically investigate the efficacy of this approach, this study addresses the following Research Questions (RQs):
* **RQ1:** Does a forward-looking, episodic reformulation resolve the structural "behavioral dominance" and target leakage prevalent in historical churn definitions?
* **RQ2:** Do LLM-derived semantic trajectories provide statistically significant, complementary predictive value when evaluated under an unconstrained survival architecture?
* **RQ3:** Can these semantically refined survival predictions be translated into a financially viable, deployable Decision Support System (DSS) compared to historically biased approaches?

The core contributions of this study are threefold. **First (Methodological)**, we expose and mathematically resolve the structural target leakage that plagues NLP-integrated e-commerce survival models, introducing a reproducible Universal episodic framework. **Second (Empirical)**, we provide rigorous statistical evidence—via $1,000$ paired bootstrap iterations—demonstrating that LLM semantic features transition from being utterly useless in historical frameworks ($\Delta$ C-index $\approx 0$) to highly significant predictors in our proposed framework ($\Delta$ C-index $= +0.159$). **Third (Practical)**, we bridge the gap between predictive and prescriptive analytics by simulating a budget-constrained Decision Support System (DSS), proving that our architectural shift transforms biologically implausible "perfect" predictions into realistic, deployable business strategies yielding tangible ROI.
